# Studio to project wall — storyboard 01

## Revision 02: a living scene and expressive menu

The user likes the drawn art style and broadly accepts the transition, but
clarified that the environment must keep moving after a section opens. Rain,
monitor activity, and character movement were examples of the desired feeling,
not a prescribed asset list. The new supplied Persona screenshot directs the
menu toward irregular oversized lettering, angled black shapes, and a bright
layered selection highlight instead of uniform strips.

The second playable study adds continuous rain, changing decorative monitor
lines, breathing, small head and hand movements, blinking, steam, and activity
on the wall display. The reading surfaces stay still. The scene responds subtly
to pointer position. Ambient animation pauses offscreen, when the page is hidden,
or when the user pauses it. Reduced motion starts it paused, with an explicit
preview control to enable it. These are staging examples, not locked requirements
for every future section.

## What this review shows

An authored, playable scene study with a generic stand-in character. It tests the
room composition, a character turn and reach, the camera framing, project
selection, and entry into an Active Directory explanation. The main menu's other
sections are visibly marked as later scenes. HiveMind and FlexiFit have readable
selection previews; only Active Directory has an interactive breakdown here.

The proposed setting is a nighttime studio, not a confirmed personal location.
The character is not a likeness of Kamaldeep and is not final character design.
The hand action, camera movement, and room layout are the decisions to review.
Playback is deliberately slow enough to inspect; it is not the final menu latency.

## Six moments

1. **Studio.** Character by a workbench; large lower-left menu; project wall to the right.
2. **Turn.** Selecting Targets draws the character's attention to the project wall.
3. **Reach.** The character reaches toward the display; its project structure lights up.
4. **Follow.** The camera tracks toward the wall as the main menu recedes.
5. **Project wall.** Character steps aside on desktop, leaving room for project selection and a readable summary.
6. **Inside the build.** The scene adds a conceptual network breakdown. Selecting Sites, Replication, or Policy changes the explanation and diagram emphasis.

Back returns to the room. A six-frame selector lets the user inspect each moment,
and Play runs through the complete sequence. Phone layout gives the scene its own
upper area, with the menu beneath and project details occupying the available width.

## Before final production

- Settle whether this room and character-led transition express the intended experience.
- Choose the character's identity, clothing, silhouette, and finished drawing style.
- Establish the finished illustration or stylized 3D workflow and asset source.
- Produce coordinated poses and environments rather than stretching one portrait.
- Refine the camera and physical hand-to-display interaction with finished assets.
- Storyboard Experience, Skills, Status, and Contact as distinct character activities.
